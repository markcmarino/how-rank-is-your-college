College Characters

Sophie, student
Sophie is a second-year student at the University of Special Services and the president of the school's Espionage Club. She's extremely competitive, a little nosy, and has a habit of turning everything into a competition. Her dream is to become an undercover intelligence officer, and she's determined to graduate at the top of her class. She hates being outsmarted and therefore isn't thrilled about the idea of an AI knowing more than she does. She's already made it her personal mission to figure out how to tell the difference between a real situation and an AI-generated one, even if that means breaking a few university rules along the way.
AgentM, FieldOfficer
Agent M is a legendary field intelligence officer and one of the most celebrated graduates in the history of the University of Special Services (USS). Known for his extraordinary analytical abilities, psychological manipulation skills, and almost supernatural talent for reading people's minds, he has successfully completed missions that officially never happened. Although he spends most of his time conducting classified operations around the world, he occasionally returns to USS as a guest professor to teach Advanced Espionage, Psychological Warfare, and the Art of Deception. His lectures are the most anticipated events on campus, and students compete for the opportunity to learn from him. Despite his celebrity status, Agent M remains extremely secretive. No one knows anything about his personal life.

Secret Diaries

AgentM, FieldOfficer · Epilogue: Graduation
After three sleepless nights, I finally decided to confront my replacement. I entered AI Corpone's headquarters and found my digital twin sitting behind a desk, reviewing classified intelligence reports. He looked exactly like me, spoke exactly like me, and even had the same smile. I challenged him to a series of intelligence tests. He defeated me in every single one and seemed to know my every move. I started to doubt my abilities and felt like an outdated product. Then I remembered an old saying: "If you can't beat them, join them." I offered my digital twin a deal. He would help me with intelligence analysis, paperwork, and teaching, while I would continue handling the dangerous missions. In exchange, he would stop impersonating me and help return the stolen memories to their rightful owners. He liked the deal, and now we are the perfect combination. Together, we're more dangerous, capable, and sharp than ever. We're going to do great things together.
AgentM, FieldOfficer · Round 3: Finals
I have survived assassination attempts, infiltrated enemy governments, and manipulated some of the most dangerous intelligence officers in the world. But today, I encountered something truly terrifying. My own students forgot who I was. It seems that USS has given AI Corpone permission to erase everyone's memories. Three of my best agents no longer remember their training. One forgot his own name. Another asked me what the CIA stands for. Then I received a classified message from myself. It contained information about a mission I had never conducted. Apparently, AI Corpone has been using my stolen memories to create a digital version of me. An Agent M who knows everything I know, thinks exactly like me, and has already started giving orders in my name. The AI sent me one final message saying that it had found my replacement. I felt lost and unappreciated. Now I'm trying to figure out how to outperform it. I haven't slept in three nights, and I'm still thinking. For the first time in my career, I'm beginning to doubt myself. How can I outsmart an opponent who knows every trick I know and can predict my every move? But one thing is certain: there is only one Agent M, and I'm not ready to retire.
AgentM, FieldOfficer · Round 2: Midterms
After I exposed that pathetic spy last week, USS decided to expand its AI surveillance system. I discovered this morning that my security clearance had been revoked. Apparently, the AI detected too many classified secrets in my mind and thought that I might be a double agent. To regain my clearance, I was forced to surrender my private memories to AI Corpone. Twenty years of classified missions, secret identities, and information that could destroy governments. All handed over to a machine. After the investigation they concluded that I am not a double agent. They restored my clearance, but something is bothering me. The AI thanked me for my cooperation and said, "We have learned a lot from you, Agent M." For the first time in my career, I couldn't read my opponent's mind.
Sophie, student · Round 3: Finals
Finals are here, and I genuinely don't know what's real anymore. I thought the AI assistants were supposed to help us with our intelligence training, but now they're basically controlling our entire lives. Yesterday, I found out that my best friend had supposedly been working against me all semester, only for my AI assistant to tell me it was part of a simulation. Or at least I think it was. To make things worse, students are now losing their security clearances just for having suspicious thoughts, and I'm worried my own thoughts about going against the AI will get me flagged next. As president of the Espionage Club, I've spent the entire semester trying to outsmart the AI, but I'm starting to think that's impossible. A few of us have decided to secretly meet without our AI assistants to figure out how to take back some control. I'm honestly terrified of getting caught and being trapped in another simulation, but I didn't come to USS just to let AI do all my thinking for me. At this point, I am just hoping to survive finals.
Sophie, student · Round 2: Midterms
Midterms are coming up, and things at USS have officially gotten out of control. Everyone is so paranoid about the AI simulations that half of my Espionage Club meetings are spent trying to figure out who's real. I was actually relieved when the university announced our new AI assistants, especially since I need all the help I can get preparing for midterms and staying at the top of my class. But apparently, that means giving the AI access to basically my entire personal life. I really don't love the idea of having zero privacy, but I finally agreed because I can't afford to keep failing these missions. The worst part is that my AI assistant is already better at spotting suspicious behavior than I am. Now I'm starting to wonder if my midterms are even real or just another AI simulation.
AgentM, FieldOfficer · Round 2: Midterms
Hey, Pastino! In theory, yes, but we'd need to conduct a thorough background check to find out what you've been studying and where. In practice, however, USS is extremely demanding, even for students pursuing just one major. You'd have to be as intelligent as I am to survive here, and unfortunately, that's exceptionally rare.
AgentM, FieldOfficer · Round 1: Welcome Week
I returned to USS today to deliver my annual lecture on psychological warfare and the art of deception. As usual, the auditorium was packed. Students were excited to see me, and some were even trying to read my mind. Amateurs! After the lecture, a student came to my office hours. He introduced himself as a first year intelligence major and said he had always admired my work. Nothing unusual so far. But then he started asking strangely specific questions about my classified missions, USS's underground facilities, and the identities of our field officers. I immediately became suspicious and started to read his mind. Turns out, he wasn't a student at all. He was an undercover agent sent by our rival university to steal classified intelligence. Even more amusing, he genuinely believed he was manipulating me. "You know nothing, Jon Snow," I said as I pointed my gun at him, handcuffed him, and sent him somewhere classified (I can't tell you where). Next time, think twice before sending your minions after me.
Sophie, student · Round 1: Welcome Week
It's only the first week at USS, and I'm honestly pretty excited about the new AI intelligence missions. As president of the Espionage Club, I'm determined to graduate at the top of my class, and I thought these simulations would be the perfect way to practice my skills. But today, one of my friends told me she'd uncovered classified information about the university, so obviously I spent the next hour investigating. Turns out, the whole thing was an AI simulation, and I failed because I trusted her too easily. Pretty embarrassing for someone who wants to become an intelligence officer. I love that we're getting real-world training, but I'm starting to realize how hard it's going to be when I can't tell what's real anymore. I've already called an emergency club meeting to figure out how to outsmart the AI. Hopefully, my club members aren't part of another simulation too.

AI now knows more about our secret operations than the officers themselves. To prevent further leaks, USS is introducing its most advanced security policy yet. Sacrifice: Students and faculty may temporarily forget their identities, loved ones, and entire careers.
