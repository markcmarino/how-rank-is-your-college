College Characters

Sophie, student
Sophie is a second-year student at the University of Special Services and the president of the school's Espionage Club. She's extremely competitive, a little nosy, and has a habit of turning everything into a competition. Her dream is to become an undercover intelligence officer, and she's determined to graduate at the top of her class. She hates being outsmarted and therefore isn't thrilled about the idea of an AI knowing more than she does. She's already made it her personal mission to figure out how to tell the difference between a real situation and an AI-generated one, even if that means breaking a few university rules along the way.
AgentM, FieldOfficer
Agent M is a legendary field intelligence officer and one of the most celebrated graduates in the history of the University of Special Services (USS). Known for his extraordinary analytical abilities, psychological manipulation skills, and almost supernatural talent for reading people's minds, he has successfully completed missions that officially never happened. Although he spends most of his time conducting classified operations around the world, he occasionally returns to USS as a guest professor to teach Advanced Espionage, Psychological Warfare, and the Art of Deception. His lectures are the most anticipated events on campus, and students compete for the opportunity to learn from him. Despite his celebrity status, Agent M remains extremely secretive. No one knows anything about his personal life.

Secret Diaries

AgentM, FieldOfficer · Round 1: Welcome Week
I returned to USS today to deliver my annual lecture on psychological warfare and the art of deception. As usual, the auditorium was packed. Students were excited to see me, and some were even trying to read my mind. Amateurs! After the lecture, a student came to my office hours. He introduced himself as a first year intelligence major and said he had always admired my work. Nothing unusual so far. But then he started asking strangely specific questions about my classified missions, USS's underground facilities, and the identities of our field officers. I immediately became suspicious and started to read his mind. Turns out, he wasn't a student at all. He was an undercover agent sent by our rival university to steal classified intelligence. Even more amusing, he genuinely believed he was manipulating me. "You know nothing, Jon Snow," I said as I pointed my gun at him, handcuffed him, and sent him somewhere classified (I can't tell you where). Next time, think twice before sending your minions after me.
Sophie, student · Round 1: Welcome Week
It's only the first week at USS, and I'm honestly pretty excited about the new AI intelligence missions. As president of the Espionage Club, I'm determined to graduate at the top of my class, and I thought these simulations would be the perfect way to practice my skills. But today, one of my friends told me she'd uncovered classified information about the university, so obviously I spent the next hour investigating. Turns out, the whole thing was an AI simulation, and I failed because I trusted her too easily. Pretty embarrassing for someone who wants to become an intelligence officer. I love that we're getting real-world training, but I'm starting to realize how hard it's going to be when I can't tell what's real anymore. I've already called an emergency club meeting to figure out how to outsmart the AI. Hopefully, my club members aren't part of another simulation too.

Policies

Following our partnership with AI Corpone, the University of Special Services is introducing a new policy that replaces traditional exams and lectures with AI-generated intelligence missions. Instead of learning through textbooks, students will participate in realistic scenarios involving espionage, undercover operations, interrogations, and national security threats. The AI will track students' decisions and create personalized challenges to help them improve their analytical and problem-solving skills. However, to make the training as realistic as possible, students will no longer be told when they are participating in a simulation.
