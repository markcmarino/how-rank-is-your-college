College Fair Pitch

The Institute for Bold Beginnings is a treehouse village in a forest that grows a new branch for every finished project, so our campus gets bigger every year. Our specialties include Architecture, Sculpting with Wood, Song Composition, Ecological Storytelling, and Designing Things That Grow. Every student plants something at orientation, and that plant is theirs to keep until graduation. We're known for graduates who plant an idea and watch it turn into something the whole forest can use.

College Characters

Juniper, student
Juniper is a second-year Song Composition student who writes melodies by humming into the hollow of her dorm tree to hear how they echo. Her orientation plant is a little maidenhair fern named Verse, which she waters every morning and sings to every night. She has started forty songs and finished zero, so she has no branch yet. Her goal this year: finish one real song and finally grow her first branch.
Hollis, arborist
Hollis has been the Institute's head arborist for 26 years. He knows which branch came from which student's project, and he can tell a rushed project from a loved one just by the bark. He keeps hand written notes on every tree in a stack of soggy notebooks. His goal this year: keep the forest healthy, keep the treehouses standing, and train an apprentice before his knees give out.

Hollis, arborist · Round 3: Finals
The fiberglass canopy went up and the real forest went dark. Kids are refusing to come down from their branches. The drones don't know what to do with a student hugging a branch, so they just hover and beep. I've been sneaking out at night with my grafting knife. Every human branch they mark, I take a cutting and graft it onto a sapling outside the fence. I've got 62 so far. No sensors, no tags. It's chaos out there. It's the most useful I've felt in years.
Juniper, student · Round 3: Finals
I finished it. A real song, by me, with a bridge I wrote at 3am, and a real branch grew. Small, crooked, and it has LEAVES. Two days later, there's an orange tag on it: SCHEDULED FOR REMOVAL. So I climbed it. I've been sitting up here for six hours singing my song while the drones circle. Other students heard and started climbing their own human branches. There are like thirty of us in the trees now, singing different songs at the same time. It sounds terrible, but it sounds amazing.

They sent pruning drones. Twelve of them, buzzing around cutting whatever their math says to cut. They took a branch off the music hall that's been there since 1987, a student's whole senior project. Every tree I've loved is in their system now. I went home and couldn't sleep. I kept hearing buzzing.
Juniper, student · Round 2: Midterms

I put my hand on the Great Oak this morning and it was warm. Trees aren't supposed to be warm. Corpone sent me a tablet to "log branch metrics." I logged them in my notebook instead. The tablet keeps asking me to rate my satisfaction. I gave it a two.

Policies

EXTREME MEASURE: The Clear Canopy Initiative. The forest is now so crowded with AI branches that no sunlight reaches the ground, and the Great Oak has developed a crack our partners describe as "concerning." To make room for growth, all "unproductive" branches (those grown from slow, human-made projects) will be removed. All future projects will grow on the Synthetic Canopy, a fiberglass forest installed above the real one. Boon: unlimited, lightning-fast expansion with zero leaf cleanup. Concession: the original forest will be "retired."IFBB-3, Extreme Measure, Round 3: Finals

AMENDMENT TO POLICY 1: Branch Quality Assurance. We have received reports that AI-grown branches are hollow, leafless, and heavy enough to tilt several treehouses. Also, the Great Oak servers are running hot, and student plants near them are wilting. Effective immediately, every AI project must be "humanized" with the student's handprint before it can grow a branch. To protect plant health, all orientation plants will be moved to the new Corpone Greenhouse for professional optimization. Boon: healthier plants and better branches. Concession: students may visit their plants on Sundays, through the glass.IFBB-2, Amendment, Round 2: Midterms

Concession: Corpone's servers will be installed inside the Great Oak, our oldest tree, and every student's orientation plant will be fitted with a Corpone sensor tag.
